# LUAD vs LUSC: CD8 and TAM Biology in Neoadjuvant Anti-PD-1 Treated NSCLC

## Research Question

How does CD8 and TAM biology (transcriptional activity, subtype heterogeneity, cell-cell communication) differ between lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) in patients treated with neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 therapy?

## Dataset

- **Source**: GSE243013 (Liu et al., 2025, *Cell*), single-cell RNA/TCR-seq of surgical tumor samples post-neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy in NSCLC.
- **Cohort**: 243 patients total (63 LUAD, 180 LUSC), verified from GEO metadata by unique `sampleID` and cross-checked against the `pathological_response` distribution (MPR n=45, non-MPR n=112, pCR n=85, unknown n=1).
- **Note on cohort size**: this is slightly higher than the 234 patients reported in the original publication's abstract. The source of this discrepancy was not resolved; it does not appear related to sample-level duplication or response-category mislabeling (both were checked directly against the metadata).
- **Metadata file**: `GSE243013_NSCLC_immune_scRNA_metadata.csv.gz`, cell-level resolution, key columns include `sampleID`, `cancer_type`, `pathological_response`, `major_cell_type`, `sub_cell_type`.

**EGFR status note:** Not available in public metadata as a dedicated field.
No EGFR TKI recorded in the `targeted_therapy` field for either arm. This is consistent with the cohort's inclusion criteria as reported in the source publication (Liu et al., 2025): patients with EGFR mutation or ALK rearrangement were excluded by design, since these patients would be eligible for targeted therapies instead. The cohort can therefore be considered predominantly EGFR wild-type by construction, not merely by inference from comparable trials.

**Naming convention**: within each `Scripts/BlocX_*/` folder, scripts are numbered starting at `01`, following the execution order *within that block* (not a global pipeline count). ## Methodology

### Bloc0_DataPrep

- **Separation**: LUAD/LUSC split performed once (Script 00), producing reference ID lists (`sampleID`, `cellID`) reused by all downstream scripts. No re-filtering of `cancer_type` elsewhere in the pipeline.
- **Verification**: patient counts checked against previously validated values (63 LUAD, 180 LUSC) at each step; script halts on mismatch.
- **Matrix loading**: BPCells used for disk-backed loading, avoiding full in-memory load of the raw count matrix.
- **Clinical metadata**: one row per patient, deduplicated by `sampleID`. A known typo (`"unknowm"` instead of `"unknown"`) in `pathological_response` was kept as-is for traceability rather than silently corrected.
- **Response categories**: LUAD has 3 response categories (MPR, non-MPR, pCR). LUSC has 4, including one patient labeled `"unknowm"` (unclassified). This patient is retained in all data and score calculations, but excluded from MPR vs non-MPR statistical comparisons and main figures, same treatment as pCR (see ROADMAP.md).
- **Total cell count cross-check**: LUAD (336,685) + LUSC (918,064) = 1,254,749 cells, matching the full unfiltered metadata row count.

### Bloc1_QC

- **Metrics**: nFeature_RNA, nCount_RNA, and percent.mt (mitochondrial content, `^MT-` pattern) computed per cell, after minimal Seurat object creation filters (`min.cells=3`, `min.features=200`).
- **Threshold selection**: thresholds based on the 99th percentile of each metric's distribution, computed separately for LUAD and LUSC (never copied across histologies), and uniform across all patients and pathological response groups within each histology. Differentiating thresholds by response group was explicitly avoided, since `pathological_response` is the primary comparison axis (MPR vs non-MPR); group-specific thresholds risk introducing an artificial bias into that comparison.
- **Thresholds applied**:
  - LUAD: nFeature_RNA [200-5000], nCount_RNA [1000-28000], percent.mt < 10%
  - LUSC: nFeature_RNA [200-4300], nCount_RNA [1000-26000], percent.mt < 10%
- **Verification**: cells removed per pathological response group checked post-filtering to confirm no group was disproportionately affected. LUAD: MPR -1.8%, non-MPR -2.7%, pCR -1.4%. LUSC: MPR -3.1%, non-MPR -3.0%, pCR -2.5%, unclassified ("unknowm") -2.5%. Comparable removal rates across groups in both histologies support the validity of uniform thresholds for the downstream MPR vs non-MPR comparison.
- **Object portability**: raw counts converted from BPCells (disk-backed) to standard in-memory sparse matrix (`dgCMatrix`) at the end of QC filtering, ensuring downstream objects remain portable across operating systems/environments, independent of the original BPCells storage path.
- **Response group filtering**: following QC filtering, cells are restricted to `pathological_response %in% c("MPR", "non-MPR")`. pCR patients (and the single "unknowm"-labeled LUSC patient) are excluded from this point onward in both histologies, since neither is used in any downstream scoring or comparison beyond a descriptive pCR/total ratio per histology (reported separately from clinical metadata, not from the filtered Seurat object). Cell counts after filtering: LUAD 268,615 cells (MPR 50,914 / non-MPR 217,701, from 328,854 before filtering); LUSC 478,223 cells (MPR 183,956 / non-MPR 294,267, from 892,413 before filtering).

### Bloc2_Norm_HVG_PCA

- **Normalization**: LogNormalize (scale factor 10,000), standard method.
- **Highly variable genes**: 3,000 HVGs for both LUAD and LUSC (vst method), above the Seurat default of 2,000. Verified against `major_cell_type` metadata (CD45+ sorted dataset, no epithelial compartment), intended to better capture fine-grained heterogeneity within immune subpopulations. LUSC initially used a reduced HVG count (2,000) as a temporary computational constraint on the larger pre-filtering dataset; unified to 3,000 (matching LUAD) once the response-group filtering (see Bloc1_QC) reduced the LUSC cell count enough to remove this constraint.
- **Cell cycle regression**: not applied to either histology. Initially applied to LUAD only, then reassessed: regression reduced but did not fully resolve a mixed-lineage proliferation cluster (a residual mixed cluster still required post-hoc resolution via SingleR - see Bloc5), so it did not provide a complete solution even where it succeeded technically. On LUSC, regression additionally caused repeated memory allocation failures on the larger dataset. Since post-hoc SingleR resolution alone proved sufficient on LUAD, regression was dropped entirely for consistency across both histologies.
- **Scaling**: applied to HVGs only, not the full gene set, to limit memory usage. The dense scaled matrix (`scale.data`) is discarded from the object immediately after PCA, since it is not required by any downstream step (Harmony, clustering, UMAP, `FindAllMarkers`, SingleR all operate on the PCA/Harmony embedding or on the normalized sparse matrix).
- **PCA**: 50 components computed for both arms; number of PCs to retain determined from per-PC variance explained, using the point where successive PCs contribute a near-negligible marginal variance (<0.1%).
- **PC1 diagnostic**: correlation with nCount_RNA checked to rule out a purely technical origin for the dominant component (LUAD: r=-0.50, 30.8% variance; LUSC: r=0.48, 28.9% variance - both moderate, not indicative of a purely technical artifact); correlation with percent.mt negligible in both arms (LUAD r≈-0.02; LUSC r≈-0.06).
- **PCs retained**: 20 for both LUAD and LUSC, slightly beyond the strict elbow (~PC12-13) to reduce the risk of losing signal from rare subpopulations of interest (TAM, CD8 substates). Cumulative 20-PC variance: 82.4% (LUAD), 82.4% (LUSC).

### Bloc3_Harmony

- **Batch correction**: Harmony (`group.by.vars = "sampleID"`), correcting for inter-patient technical variation, applied on the 20 PCs retained in Bloc2.
- **Verification**: patient-level dispersion (SD of per-patient mean coordinate) compared before/after Harmony as a proxy for integration quality. LUAD: 5.93→5.40 (-8.9%). LUSC: 5.53→5.03 (-9.0%). Comparable reductions across both histologies support the robustness of the integration step.

### Bloc4_Clustering_UMAP

- **Clustering**: `FindNeighbors`/`FindClusters` on the Harmony embedding (not raw PCA), 20 dimensions, resolution 0.5 as a conservative starting point for global TME clustering (17 clusters LUAD; 21 clusters LUSC).
- **UMAP**: computed on the Harmony embedding, same 20 dimensions.
- **Integration quality check**: patient distribution visually inspected on the UMAP; well-mixed colors across all major cell islands in both LUAD and LUSC.

### Bloc5_Annotation_TME

**LUAD**

- **Marker-based annotation**: `FindAllMarkers` (Wilcoxon test via `presto`), top10 markers per cluster reviewed manually against canonical immune markers. 17 clusters (filtered MPR/non-MPR object, no cell-cycle regression) reviewed as an initial exploratory pass; not merged into the final annotation (see below).
- **Cell-level annotation (CellTypist)**: `Immune_All_Low` model (Domínguez Conde et al., 2022, *Science*; multi-tissue atlas including tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes), run with `majority_voting = True`. Applied systematically to all 17 clusters; a cluster was confirmed directly when ≥95% of its cells shared a CellTypist label within a single biological family. This directly confirmed 12/17 clusters. Direct comparison against an initial SingleR (MonacoImmuneData) cross-check on the same 17 clusters showed CellTypist achieving broader and cleaner direct confirmation (12/17 vs. 11/17 clusters confirmed directly). Mixed clusters requiring cell-level resolution also showed markedly fewer sub-groups with CellTypist: after filtering out statistically unreliable labels, 8-12 sub-groups per cluster (CellTypist) versus up to 14-15 (SingleR), out of 8-12 versus up to 22-25 raw distinct labels respectively. This is consistent with SingleR/MonacoImmuneData's peripheral-blood origin being less suited to tissue-infiltrating and closely related T-cell states. CellTypist was therefore adopted as the sole cell-level annotation method.
- **Mixed cluster resolution**: for the 5 remaining clusters (1, 2, 3, 6, 11), cells were grouped by their individual `CellTypist_majority` label, then a targeted `FindAllMarkers` was run per subgroup. Each subgroup's marker profile was checked against the CellTypist authors' own curated marker sets (Mullan et al., 2023, *Front Immunol* for T-cell subtypes; Domínguez Conde et al., 2022 for NK/myeloid/B) — a subgroup was confirmed only if its official marker(s) were recovered among its own top differentially expressed genes; unconfirmed subgroups were retained as explicitly traceable "Unresolved" labels rather than assigned an unverified identity. Signals dominated by non-immune markers (lung epithelial contamination: *SCGB1A1*, *SFTPB*, *SFTPC*, *SCGB3A1*) were excluded as non-immune contamination — a pattern independently reported by the CellTypist authors as an ambient-RNA/doublet artifact in their own macrophage cluster analysis.
- **Final annotation** (`annotation_final`): built entirely from CellTypist. Each retained label is the model's own terminology, not translated into project-specific nomenclature, to preserve direct traceability to the source classifier. 268,615 cells, 0 unannotated. 86.5% of cells received a marker-confirmed identity; 13.0% remained "Unresolved" and 0.6% were excluded as non-immune contamination. Technical lineage flags (`lineage_CD8`, `lineage_TAM`, `lineage_TAM_related`) were built from confirmed labels only, for downstream Bloc6/Bloc7 extraction.
- **Display simplification**: for figure readability only, categories below 500 cells are grouped into a single "Minor/unresolved categories" bin, and non-immune contamination into a separate bin; this applies to the summary figure (`annotation_display`) only and does not affect `annotation_final`, which retains every individually confirmed or unconfirmed label and is used for all statistical tests and downstream analyses.
- **Statistical comparison (patient-level)**: per-patient proportions of the two compartments characterised downstream (`lineage_TAM`, `lineage_CD8_revised`) compared between MPR and non-MPR using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the two populations. Patients contributing fewer than 50 annotated cells were excluded: a proportion computed over a very small denominator is unstable regardless of the population's abundance, since one cell out of twelve already represents eight percent. This threshold shares its value but not its rationale with the 50-cell minimum used in Bloc6, which guarded against false zeros for a rare state rather than against unstable proportions. This supersedes an earlier cell-level approach (global chi-squared plus targeted `prop.test`), which reported significant differences that did not survive patient-level testing — see the note on pseudoreplication below.
- **Composition figure**: three panels per histology. Panel A is the cumulative composition barplot, retained as a descriptive overview but with no test attached, since cells from one patient are not independent observations. Panels B and C are per-patient boxplots with individual points, for CD8 T cells and macrophages respectively. The figure thus displays the problem it corrects: an apparent contrast in A, absent in B and C. Memory B cells produce the most visually striking contrast in panel A and were tested at the patient level in an earlier exploration (LUAD 31.7 versus 16.8 percent, P = 0.038; LUSC not significant), but are not reported: mentioning them without carrying them into the downstream analyses would raise the question of why they were not chosen, and the perimeter of this work is CD8 and TAM. The figure legend states explicitly that other populations visible in panel A were not tested.
- **LUSC alveolar label grouped for display**: CellTypist assigns 16,273 of 16,920 LUSC macrophages to an alveolar macrophage label. These cells are displayed with macrophages rather than as a separate category, since marker examination identified them as recruited rather than tissue-resident TAMs (see Bloc7). The `lineage_TAM` flag already covered both labels, so the tests were correct before this display change.

**LUSC**

- **Marker-based annotation**: `FindAllMarkers` (Wilcoxon test via `presto`), top10 markers per cluster reviewed manually against canonical immune markers. 21 clusters (filtered MPR/non-MPR object, no cell-cycle regression) reviewed as an initial exploratory pass; not merged into the final annotation.
- **Patient composition check**: during marker-based cluster review, one myeloid cluster (391 cells) showed an unexpected *XIST* signal in its top differentially expressed genes — a sex-linked marker with no lineage-specific relevance, prompting a check of patient composition. This cluster was found to originate entirely from a single patient (100%); the patient's overall cell count and QC metrics were within normal range across other clusters, and the pattern was attributed to a residual Harmony integration artifact for this specific cell subset rather than a quality issue. The patient was retained in the analysis without modification; CellTypist confirmed a biologically plausible identity (Classical monocytes) for this cluster.
- **Cell-level annotation (CellTypist)**: `Immune_All_Low` model (Domínguez Conde et al., 2022, *Science*), run with `majority_voting = True`, applied identically to LUSC as to LUAD. Applied systematically to all 21 clusters using the same ≥95% single-family concordance threshold. This directly confirmed 13/21 clusters; the remaining 8 (clusters 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12, 14, 20) mixed multiple cell states and required resolution at the single-cell level.
- **Mixed cluster resolution**: for each of the 8 mixed clusters, cells were grouped by their individual `CellTypist_majority` label, then a targeted `FindAllMarkers` was run per subgroup. Each subgroup's marker profile was checked against the CellTypist authors' own curated marker sets (Mullan et al., 2023; Domínguez Conde et al., 2022) — a subgroup was confirmed only if its official marker(s) were recovered among its own top differentially expressed genes; unconfirmed subgroups were retained as explicitly traceable "Unresolved" labels. A recurrent lung epithelial contamination signal (*SCGB1A1*, *SFTPC*, *SFTPA1*, *SFTPB*) was identified within otherwise immune-labeled subgroups in several clusters and excluded, consistent with the pattern observed in LUAD and with the ambient-RNA/doublet artifact independently reported by the CellTypist authors.
- **Final annotation** (`annotation_final`): built entirely from CellTypist, using the model's own terminology without translation to project-specific nomenclature. 478,223 cells, 0 unannotated. One resolved cluster (391 cells, confirmed as Classical monocytes) originated entirely from a single patient and is flagged as a Harmony integration artifact rather than a distinct biological subpopulation, though its cell-level identity remains biologically plausible. Technical lineage flags (`lineage_CD8`, `lineage_TAM`, `lineage_TAM_related`) were built from marker-confirmed labels only, for downstream Bloc6/Bloc7 extraction; LUSC additionally distinguishes "Macrophages" from "Alveolar macrophages" as two separately confirmed labels, both included in `lineage_TAM`.
- **Display simplification and statistical comparison**: identical approach to LUAD (see above) — display-only grouping of small/unresolved categories, and patient-level Wilcoxon testing of the same two compartments. Neither differed (TAM: median 1.23 vs 2.46 %, adjusted P = 0.65; CD8: 22.0 vs 23.3 %, adjusted P = 0.97; 35 MPR vs 70 non-MPR).

### Bloc6_CD8

- **CD8 purification strategy**: rather than subsetting on the CellTypist-derived `lineage_CD8` flag, CD8 T cells were re-isolated independently on the full TME object of each histology using scGate (`TME_HiRes` model, human), following the same isolation-then-projection strategy used in the official construction of the human CD8 TIL reference atlas (Andreatta et al., 2021, *Nat Commun*; atlas construction pipeline: scGate → UCell cycling/ISG+ removal → STACAS). This avoids circularity with the project's own CellTypist-based annotation and keeps downstream steps methodologically consistent with the reference space used for later ProjecTILs projection.
- **Performance note**: scGate recalculates a dimensionality reduction at each hierarchical gating level by default; supplying the pre-computed Harmony embedding (`reduction = "harmony"`) instead avoids this recomputation and was necessary for tractable runtime on datasets of this size.
- **Cross-check against CellTypist**: `scGate_multi == "CD8T"` (not `lineage_CD8`, and not an intersection/union of the two) was retained as the base population carried forward to the next step, to preserve consistency with the reference atlas construction protocol; `lineage_CD8` is retained as an annotation/cross-validation column only. Concordance between the two methods was assessed but not used to filter cells at this stage; biological interpretation of any discordance is deferred until after ProjecTILs projection, which uses a CD8-specific reference atlas.
- **Cycling/IFN-high cell removal (UCell)**: applied to the scGate-confirmed CD8T population, using the identical signatures and thresholds from the official CD8 human TIL atlas construction pipeline (`cellCycle.G1S`, `cellCycle.G2M` from SignatuR, Tirosh et al., 2016, *Science*; a manually-defined 4-gene IFN signature, *ISG15, IFI6, IFI44L, MX1*, reported by the atlas authors to perform better with UCell than the full SignatuR IFN signature). Thresholds: `cellCycle.G1S < 0.1`, `cellCycle.G2M < 0.1`, `IFN < 0.25`.
- **ProjecTILs functional-state projection**: the purified, non-cycling CD8 population was projected onto the official human CD8 TIL reference atlas (Andreatta et al., 2021, *Nat Commun*; atlas construction: `carmonalab/CD8_human_TIL_atlas_construction`), using `Run.ProjecTILs()` with `filter.cells = FALSE` (query already pre-purified, per official guidance for pre-filtered data) and `split.by = "sampleID"` (per-patient batch correction, as recommended).
- **Cross-validation and identity correction**: `functional.cluster` (ProjecTILs) was crossed against `annotation_final` (CellTypist) to assess concordance between the two independent methods. Where a discrepancy affected a substantial number of cells, identity was arbitrated using raw marker expression (mean log-normalized expression and detection rate) for lineage-defining genes, rather than relying on either method's label alone; detection rate alone was treated with caution given ambient RNA/dropout noise in sparse single-cell data, and mean expression amplitude was used as the primary criterion. Corrections were applied to a CD8-lineage flag used for downstream Bloc6/Bloc7 analyses only, without retroactively altering the Bloc5 `annotation_final` labels or TME composition statistics already computed from them.
- **CD8 composition by pathological response**: within the revised CD8 lineage, per-patient proportions of each ProjecTILs functional state were compared between MPR and non-MPR tumors using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction applied across the seven states. Patients with fewer than 50 CD8 cells were excluded from this analysis, since rare states (such as CD8.TPEX, ~1–3% of CD8) have a high probability of a false zero from under-sampling alone below this threshold. This patient-level approach follows Hu et al. (2023, *Genome Medicine*), a comparable neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 NSCLC scRNA-seq study with a similarly small and unbalanced MPR/non-MPR cohort, and supersedes an earlier cell-level chi-squared/`prop.test` approach (see note below). Color coding reused the official ProjecTILs atlas palette; p-value display was standardized using `scales::pvalue()`.
- **Note on statistical unit (pseudoreplication)**: cell-level tests (chi-squared, `prop.test`) treat each cell as an independent observation despite within-patient correlation, which can inflate statistical power. This was identified while evaluating `propeller` (Phipson et al., 2022, *Bioinformatics*) on the Bloc5 TME composition data, where a cell-level-significant result did not survive patient-level testing. All composition and functional-scoring comparisons in this project were subsequently re-run at the patient level, including the Bloc5 TME composition analysis itself (see above). That re-analysis is instructive: the cell-level tests had reported a three-fold enrichment of macrophages in non-MPR LUAD tumors and an enrichment in MPR LUSC tumors, i.e. an apparent reversal of direction between histologies. At the patient level neither difference is significant, and the medians point in the same direction in both histologies. The reversal was an artefact of treating tens of thousands of cells as independent observations. The pipeline is now patient-level from end to end, with no exception. A formal interaction test (histology × response) was considered but not pursued, given the absence of precedent in comparable published studies, including the original dataset publication (Liu et al., 2025, *Cell*); LUAD/LUSC comparisons are presented descriptively.
- **Functional scoring within CD8.TEX (UCell)**: to quantify the intensity of specific functional programs beyond the discrete ProjecTILs states, three short published gene signatures (cytotoxicity: *GZMB, PRF1, FASLG*; exhaustion: *HAVCR2, CTLA4, PDCD1, TIGIT, LAG3*; stemness: *TCF7, SELL, LEF1, IL7R*; Carmona et al., 2020, *Oncoimmunology*, retrieved via `SignatuR$Hs$Programs$Tcell.*`) were scored with UCell within the CD8.TEX population, restricted to the revised CD8 lineage. These signatures were chosen over an alternative source (`signaturesHumanTILs`, ProjecTILs atlas-derived state signatures also available from the Carmona lab) because the latter largely reproduce the discrete states already assigned by ProjecTILs itself, whereas the SignatuR programs provide a continuous, independent readout of functional intensity within those states. Per-patient median scores were compared between MPR and non-MPR using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction. Patients contributing fewer than 10 CD8.TEX cells were excluded, following the default filtering used in muscat (Crowell et al., 2020), since a median computed over very few cells is unreliable; this retained 47 LUAD patients (9 MPR, 38 non-MPR) and 93 LUSC patients (29 MPR, 64 non-MPR). Note that this threshold differs from the 50-cell minimum used for the composition analysis because the two apply to different denominators: composition requires enough total CD8 cells to compute a proportion, whereas functional scoring requires enough CD8.TEX cells specifically to compute a reliable median within that state.
- **Exclusion of CD8.TPEX from functional analyses**: applying the same 10-cell minimum, only 4 MPR patients would have qualified for CD8.TPEX in LUAD, compared with 13 in LUSC. Analysing this state in LUSC alone would have introduced an asymmetric analytical framework between histologies, precluding any meaningful cross-histology comparison, so CD8.TPEX was excluded from both — neither tested nor reported descriptively, to avoid combining tested and untested results within a single argument. This focus on the terminally exhausted state is also consistent with its reported clinical relevance: baseline CD8.TEX proportion has been shown to be higher in non-responders than responders prior to immunotherapy and to independently predict checkpoint inhibitor resistance (Fang et al., 2026).
- **Transcription factor activity within CD8.TEX (CollecTRI)**: to complement gene-signature scoring with a network-based measure of regulatory activity, transcription factor (TF) activity was inferred using `decoupleR`'s univariate linear model method (`run_ulm`) with the CollecTRI network (Müller-Dott et al., 2023, *Nucleic Acids Research*; ~1,183 human TFs, downloaded directly from static.omnipathdb.org after `decoupleR::get_collectri()` failed due to an unresolved package issue, reformatted to match the expected input, with directionally-ambiguous interactions, 0.4% of the network, excluded). Rather than testing each TF's cell-level activity across patients (which yielded only 5 significant TFs out of 755 tested in an initial analysis, given limited power at n=9-10 MPR patients), a pseudobulk approach was used: raw counts were aggregated per patient within CD8.TEX, a differential expression analysis was run with DESeq2 (MPR vs non-MPR), and `run_ulm` was applied once to the resulting per-gene differential statistic. This approach, recommended in decoupleR's own documentation for this type of comparison, substantially increased sensitivity (67 of 637 TFs significant in LUAD, 10.5%, versus 14 of 649 in LUSC, 2.2%) without introducing repeated per-TF testing across patients. No TFs were pre-selected; all those passing the network's minimum target-gene coverage (`minsize = 5`) were tested.
- **CD8.TPEX and TF activity**: consistent with its exclusion from signature scoring, CD8.TPEX was not analysed with CollecTRI.
- **TCR repertoire analysis within CD8.TEX**: to test whether the MPR/non-MPR asymmetry observed in the transcription factor analysis reflects a less-engaged TEX compartment in responders, clonal expansion was assessed using TCR data as a line of evidence independent of gene expression — clone size records past proliferation rather than current transcriptional state. Clonotype assignments were taken directly from the authors' annotation file accompanying GSE243013 (`GSE243013_T_with_TCR_annotation.csv.gz`), joined to the Seurat object by cell barcode; clonotype identifiers are patient-prefixed and non-overlapping across patients (verified: 255,451 clonotypes, none shared). Two complementary metrics were computed per patient within CD8.TEX: (i) the percentage of cells belonging to an expanded clone, using the ≥3-cell threshold defined by the original dataset publication (Liu et al., 2025, *Cell*, STAR Methods), and (ii) Shannon diversity (H = -Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ). Both were calculated explicitly rather than through package defaults, and the pre-computed `expansion` column of the annotation file was deliberately not reused, its underlying threshold being undocumented. The two metrics are non-redundant: the first quantifies the share of the compartment held by proliferating lineages, the second the overall balance of the repertoire.
- **Denominator and patient threshold for repertoire metrics**: TCR recovery is incomplete and varies between patients (median 74–85% of TEX cells), and several patients yielded no TCR data at all despite substantial TEX cell numbers (LUSC: 7 patients, including one with 468 TEX cells; LUAD: 2 patients). Using all TEX cells as the denominator would have assigned these patients spurious values of 0% expansion and H = 0. Both metrics were therefore computed over TCR-bearing TEX cells only, and patients were required to contribute at least 10 such cells — the same 10-cell minimum applied in the functional scoring analysis, but applied to the TCR-bearing subset rather than to all TEX cells. This retained 44 LUAD patients (9 MPR, 35 non-MPR) and 86 LUSC patients (28 MPR, 58 non-MPR). Per-patient values were compared between MPR and non-MPR using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the two metrics, consistent with all other patient-level comparisons in Bloc6.
- **Sampling-depth control and rarefaction**: Shannon diversity is systematically underestimated at low sample size, with the bias increasing as diversity rises (Konopiński, 2020, *PeerJ*). This bias was confirmed within the present data (Spearman correlation between per-patient Shannon and cell number: rho = 0.657, p = 1.3e-06 in LUAD; rho = 0.567, p = 1.3e-08 in LUSC). Sampling depth was therefore compared between response groups before interpreting any diversity difference, and found comparable in both histologies (LUAD: median 153 versus 151 TCR-bearing TEX cells, p = 0.60; LUSC: 99 versus 111, p = 0.40), meaning both groups are subject to the same downward bias and remain comparable. As a sensitivity analysis only — the status of which was fixed before the analysis was run — metrics were additionally recomputed after rarefying all patients to a common depth of 30 cells (100 random draws per patient, averaged). A third sensitivity analysis redefined clone size over all T cells rather than within CD8.TEX, since the source threshold does not specify a reference population. The unrarefied within-TEX analysis remains the primary analysis throughout.

### Bloc7_TAM

- **Population and cross-validation (scGate)**: macrophages were extracted from the CellTypist annotation (`lineage_TAM`) and cross-validated with scGate's `TME_HiRes/Macrophage` model, a marker-based hierarchical gating model that reads the expression matrix directly and is therefore independent of the annotation it validates. Classical monocytes (lineage_TAM_related) were treated as a separate population throughout: they have not engaged macrophage differentiation, so the functional signatures used below would measure states they do not possess, and scGate's own Macrophage model excludes monocytes at its final gating level. Unlike Bloc6, no cycling or ISG filtering was applied — that filtering existed to match the ProjecTILs atlas, which excludes such cells by construction, whereas Prolif-TAM and IFN-TAM are recognised TAM states (Ma et al., 2022) and no reference atlas is used here.
- **Interpretation of non-retained cells**: 76.5 percent (LUAD) and 72.0 percent (LUSC) of annotated macrophages passed the model, against 27.0 and 8.9 percent of classical monocytes. Non-retained cells were characterised rather than discarded silently: they showed macrophage markers three to four times lower (C1QA/B/C, APOE) and monocyte markers three to six times higher (S100A8/A9, FCN1, VCAN), with no difference in sequencing depth, identifying them as monocyte-to-macrophage transitional states rather than annotation errors. Functional scoring was performed on the full lineage_TAM (19,238 LUAD; 16,920 LUSC), retaining these transitional cells rather than excluding them, since they represent a stage of differentiation rather than a contaminating population. Results on gating-confirmed cells alone (14,708 LUAD; 12,175 LUSC) are reported as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Correction of the LUSC "alveolar macrophage" label**: CellTypist assigned 16,273 of 16,920 LUSC macrophages to an alveolar macrophage label, against none in LUAD — an asymmetry that would have invalidated the cross-histology comparison had it been biologically real, since alveolar macrophages are tissue-resident and developmentally distinct from monocyte-derived TAMs. Four independent checks established that these are recruited TAMs: canonical alveolar markers are low (FABP4 3.13, MARCO 5.06) while recruited-TAM markers are high (SPP1 39.60, APOE 62.98); their profile matches LUAD macrophages; and they pass the scGate model at 72.9 percent despite that model containing no alveolar marker. The label most likely reflects the CellTypist reference containing healthy-lung alveolar macrophages as its closest pulmonary category.
- **Functional scoring (UCell)**: four published signatures defining M1, M2, angiogenesis and phagocytosis phenotypes in tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells (Cheng et al., 2021, *Cell*, Table S5) were scored with UCell. Discrete subtype assignment was deliberately not attempted: Cheng et al. show that markers of pro-angiogenic TAMs vary between cancer types (SPP1+ in eight tumor types, VCAN+ in breast and melanoma, INHBA+ in stomach, FN1+ in kidney), so the function is conserved while the label is not; scoring continuous functional axes sidesteps this rather than colliding with it, and also avoids the multi-score assignment problem and the rare-category problem that had forced the exclusion of CD8.TPEX in Bloc6. Two non-standard symbols in the source table, IL23 and FASL, were mapped to IL23A and FASLG; coverage was complete (16/16, 34/34, 25/25, 4/4) in both histologies.
- **Patient-level comparison**: per-patient median scores were compared between MPR and non-MPR using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the four axes, with the same ten-cell minimum used throughout the project. Two controls were run before interpretation: the number of macrophages per patient did not differ significantly between response groups, though the LUAD medians were unequal (LUAD: 125 in MPR versus 230 in non-MPR, P = 0.068; LUSC: 82.5 versus 93, P = 0.38), and neither did the proportion of scGate-confirmed cells per patient (LUAD 65.4 versus in MPR versus 53.3 percent in non-MPR, P = 0.32; LUSC 69.5 versus 69.8 percent, P = 0.55) — the latter confirming that the compositional difference reported in Bloc5 does not reflect a differential proportion of transitional cells between groups.
- **Published subtype markers (Wang et al., 2022)**: after the analyses above, the four macrophage subtype markers reported in a treatment-naive NSCLC cohort (FABP4, FCN1, SPP1, SELENOP) were tested by assigning each cell to its highest-expressing marker. This is not a reproduction of the source method, which used unsupervised clustering rather than a threshold on four genes, and whose authors report that marker expression was not exclusive to a single cluster; the test asks only whether these markers structure the compartment here. Cells expressing none of the four were left unassigned. Patients with no cell assigned to a given marker were counted as zero percent rather than excluded, since exclusion would have removed the most extreme values and biased the comparison, the same pitfall encountered in Bloc5.
- **Metabolic programs (MSigDB Hallmark)**: four gene sets were scored with UCell to test a metabolic hypothesis, namely glycolysis, hypoxia, oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid metabolism. Selection was fixed before any computation, on the basis that the two immunosuppressive TAM subtypes described in NSCLC by Yang et al. (2021) differ by metabolism rather than by phenotype, with glycolysis characterising the SPP1-positive subtype that predominates here. The remaining forty-six sets were not tested. A directional prediction was registered in advance: higher glycolysis and hypoxia in non-MPR, higher oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid metabolism in MPR, with absence of difference on all four as the falsification criterion. This was therefore the only confirmatory framework of the three applied to this compartment; the other two were exploratory as to direction.
- **Sensitivity analysis on the scoring perimeter**: the choice of perimeter was tested rather than assumed. The question was not whether one particular result held, but whether the functional scores themselves change when transitional cells are included — a question about the measurement, applied to all four axes in both histologies. No recomputation was needed: UCell scores had been calculated on the full lineage_TAM object before filtering, and score identity across the shared cells was verified. Because widening the perimeter also changes the sample size, patients previously below the ten-cell threshold now passing it, the two effects were separated by additionally testing the wider perimeter on the restricted patient set. The full lineage_TAM is used for the scoring and for the macrophage group in Bloc8, on the grounds that transitional cells lie on the same differentiation continuum and express ligands and receptors like any other; classical monocytes remain a separate group throughout. Results on gating-confirmed cells alone are retained in full, which is what makes the widening traceable rather than opportunistic. Note that the Cheng signatures were applied to macrophage subsets in the source study, and are here applied additionally to less differentiated cells — a departure stated in the Methods, though the signatures are lists of functional genes rather than cell type markers.
- **Perimeter adopted downstream**: the full lineage_TAM is used for the scoring and for the macrophage group in Bloc8, on the grounds that transitional cells lie on the same differentiation continuum and express ligands and receptors like any other. Classical monocytes remain a separate group throughout. Results on gating-confirmed cells alone are retained in full, which is what makes the widening traceable rather than opportunistic. ### Bloc8_Communication

- **Scope and rationale**: cell-cell communication was inferred with MultiNicheNet (Browaeys et al., 2023) using the NicheNet v2 prior knowledge networks (Browaeys et al., 2020), restricted to three cell groups: scGate-confirmed macrophages (Bloc7), CD8.TEX cells assigned by ProjecTILs (Bloc6), and classical monocytes (Bloc5). These are the only compartments whose biology has been characterised in this cohort, so an interaction involving any of them can be related to something established elsewhere in the project. Classical monocytes were excluded from Bloc7 because the Cheng signatures describe macrophage states, a limitation that does not apply here, and they are a substantial source and target of signalling, particularly in LUSC where they contribute 32,598 cells. CD8 cytotoxic cells were not included: adding a fourth group would double the ordered pairs tested from six to twelve, which is costly with nine MPR patients in LUAD, and their functional state was not characterised in Bloc6, so a result involving them could not be anchored in the rest of the work.

- **Two workflows exist, and the wrong one was read first**: MultiNicheNet ships a standard multi-sample vignette and a sample-agnostic one intended for fewer than three samples per group, whose authors explicitly advise against using it otherwise. This project falls under the standard workflow. The parameter values differ between them, `min_cells` being 25 and `logFC_threshold` 0.25 in the sample-agnostic version against 10 and 0.50 in the standard one. The error was identified and corrected before any analysis was run.

- **Parameters**: package defaults were kept throughout, since `min_cells = 10` is both the package default and the threshold used across this project. A gene counts as expressed if it has non-zero counts in at least 5 percent of cells of a group in a sample (`fraction_cutoff`, authors advise against exceeding 0.10) and in at least half the samples of the smaller group (`min_sample_prop = 0.50`). Differential expression uses pseudobulk with EdgeR, `logFC_threshold = 0.50`, `p_val_threshold = 0.05`. Ligand activity considers upregulated targets only (`ligand_activity_down = FALSE`), prioritisation uses the `regular` scenario weighting all criteria equally, and `top_n_target = 250`.

- **Both contrast directions must be declared explicitly**: MultiNicheNet does not test the reverse comparison automatically, so declaring a single direction would only reveal interactions specific to one group. The syntax is strict and tolerates no whitespace. Note that `make.names()` converts "non-MPR" to "non.MPR", and the contrast labels must match the converted labels exactly or the analysis fails silently; a verification step was built into the preparation script.

- **`p_val_adj = FALSE` in both histologies, against the authors' own recommendation**: they advise adjusted p-values when the smaller group reaches 20 samples, which would have applied to LUSC with 29 to 34 MPR patients. The recommendation was set aside after running the geneset-to-background ratio check that the same authors prescribe. The ligand activity step is an enrichment procedure, and the ratio of differentially expressed genes to expressed genes must lie between 1/200 and 1/10. With adjusted p-values in LUSC, three of six ratios fell outside that range, including zero differentially expressed genes in monocytes in one direction and seven in the other. With raw p-values, all six ratios lie within range. The same check confirmed the choice in LUAD, where adjusted p-values would have reduced the differential gene sets from 222 to 7 in CD8.TEX and from 365 to 14 in macrophages. This decision was taken on the data, following the tool's own procedure, and has the secondary benefit of harmonising the parameterisation across histologies.

- **Prioritisation ranks, it does not test**: the prioritisation score is a composite of differential ligand and receptor expression, cell type specificity, and predicted ligand activity, each converted to a normalised rank before averaging. It carries no significance threshold, which is a departure from the Wilcoxon-based logic used everywhere else in this project. The top 50 interactions per group were retained for interpretation, following the practice adopted in the source publication, with the cutoff fixed before examining any result and applied identically to both histologies. The top 100 was inspected as a stability check only.

- **Reference networks**: MultiNicheNet uses an annotated ligand-receptor file (`lr_network_human_allInfo`) that differs from the plain network used by NicheNet alone, a distinction easily missed. Gene aliases were converted to official symbols before `make.names()`, as prescribed in the vignette, to avoid losing genes when joining with the networks. The networks contain 5,097 ligand-receptor pairs formed of 1,234 ligands and 1,072 receptors, and a ligand-target matrix of 33,354 targets by 1,226 ligands.

- **Structural limitation**: like any communication inference from dissociated single-cell data, MultiNicheNet reads ligand and receptor expression without knowing whether the cells were physically adjacent. Two populations may express a matching pair while occupying distinct tumor regions. The interactions reported are therefore predicted, not established, and spatial transcriptomics would be the validation route.

**CD8.TPEX rarity (both histologies)**, CD8.TPEX did not receive a formal patient-level significance test, given insufficient cell counts per patient in both histologies (unrelated to pathological response; zero-cell rates were similar between MPR and non-MPR). CD8.TEX, in contrast, supported a full patient-level test.

**LUAD**

- Independent scGate-based CD8 isolation (58,203 cells) showed 72.3% agreement with the CellTypist-derived `lineage_CD8` population (57,782 cells; 41,752 cells confirmed by both methods). This level of concordance is considered acceptable at this stage given the methodological differences between the two approaches, but is not yet interpreted biologically; ProjecTILs projection (CD8-specific reference atlas) is expected to help resolve which discordant cells represent genuine CD8 states versus annotation noise from either method.
- UCell-based removal of cycling and IFN-high cells (official atlas construction thresholds) removed 8,616 cells (14.8%) from the scGate-confirmed CD8T population, leaving 49,587 cells. The IFN criterion accounted for the large majority of flagged cells (~81% of cumulative flags) relative to the cell-cycle criteria — plausibly reflecting an active immune response under neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 treatment, though this is not yet interpreted biologically at this stage.
- ProjecTILs-based functional states were dominated by CD8.EM (32.2%), CD8.TEX (30.9%), and CD8.CM (26.5%), with CD8.MAIT, CD8.TPEX, CD8.TEMRA, and CD8.NaiveLike each below 3.5%. A cross-validation step identified and corrected a CellTypist labeling error affecting 5,293 cells (10.7% of the projected CD8 population) originally annotated as regulatory T cells but confirmed as genuine CD8 T cells (predominantly exhausted/TEX) by both independent projection and raw marker expression (CD8A/CD8B high, CD4/FOXP3 near-absent). This correction is particularly relevant given the central role of the TEX/TPEX axis in this study's neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 context.
- At the patient level (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, BH-corrected across the seven states, patients with fewer than 50 CD8 cells excluded, 49/52 patients retained: 10 MPR, 39 non-MPR), no functional state reached statistical significance for the MPR versus non-MPR comparison (all BH-adjusted p ≥ 0.91). This includes CD8.TEX, which had appeared significant in an earlier cell-level analysis; the patient-level result is considered more reliable given the risk of pseudoreplication in cell-level testing (see Methodology), and the discrepancy likely reflects limited statistical power at this sample size (only 10 MPR patients) rather than the cell-level result being biologically meaningful.
- Within CD8.TEX, UCell-based scoring of cytotoxicity, exhaustion, and stemness gene programs (Carmona et al., 2020) at the patient level revealed that CD8.TEX cells in non-MPR tumors were both more cytotoxic (median 0.40 versus 0.29; BH-adjusted p = 0.004) and more exhausted (median 0.32 versus 0.18; p = 0.001) than those in MPR tumors, a pattern not mutually exclusive given that exhausted CD8 T cells are known to retain partial cytotoxic potential. The stemness score was near zero in both groups and did not differ significantly (p = 0.665), likely reflecting a known technical limitation of short gene signatures with UCell rather than a biological effect. Notably, this functional-scoring result contrasts with the corresponding composition analysis, where no CD8 functional state reached significance in LUAD at the patient level — suggesting pathological response may be more closely associated with the functional intensity of CD8.TEX cells than with their proportion within the CD8 compartment.
- Transcription factor activity inference (CollecTRI regulons applied via `run_ulm` to per-gene Wald statistics from a patient-level pseudobulk DESeq2 contrast) identified 67 of 637 tested transcription factors with significantly different activity between MPR and non-MPR tumors (10.5 percent). The strongest and most significant associations were with NF-κB (RELA, NFKB1), STAT family members (STAT1, STAT5B, STAT3), AP-1 components (JUN, FOS), HSF1, and EGR1, all more active in non-MPR tumors; only four transcription factors (ZBTB4, ZBTB14, YBX1, ZBED1) showed significantly higher activity in MPR tumors. This asymmetry mirrors the composition result, where CD8.TEX proportion did not differ significantly between conditions, suggesting pathological response is associated with the transcriptional program of TEX cells rather than their relative abundance.

**LUSC**

- Independent scGate-based CD8 isolation (108,410 cells) showed 66.4% agreement with the CellTypist-derived `lineage_CD8` population (111,596 cells; 74,133 cells confirmed by both methods), lower than LUAD (72.3%), consistent with a higher rate of CellTypist "Unresolved" cells observed in LUSC at the cross-validation step (15.2% vs. 9.2% in LUAD).
- UCell-based removal of cycling and IFN-high cells removed 12,177 cells (11.2%, lower than LUAD's 14.8%) from the scGate-confirmed CD8T population, leaving 96,233 cells. The IFN criterion still dominated (~71% of cumulative flags) but proportionally less than in LUAD (~81%), with proportionally more cell-cycle-driven removal in LUSC.
- ProjecTILs-based functional states were dominated by CD8.CM (33.8%), CD8.EM (29.2%), and CD8.TEX (23.4%), a baseline profile shifted toward less-exhausted, more terminally differentiated states compared to LUAD (CD8.EM 32.2%, CD8.TEX 30.9%, CD8.CM 26.5%). A cross-validation step identified and corrected the same CellTypist labeling error as in LUAD, affecting 6,164 cells (8.3% of the projected CD8 population) originally annotated as regulatory T cells but confirmed as genuine CD8 T cells (predominantly exhausted/TEX) by both independent projection and raw marker expression, closely mirroring the LUAD result.
- At the patient level (same approach, 101/105 patients retained: 34 MPR, 67 non-MPR), CD8.TEX proportion was significantly higher in non-MPR tumors (median 25.8% versus 11.2%; BH-adjusted p = 0.0135). CD8.CM and CD8.MAIT proportions were also significantly higher in MPR tumors (BH-adjusted p = 0.034 for both). All other states, including CD8.TPEX, were not significant after correction.
- Within CD8.TEX, UCell-based scoring at the patient level showed the same pattern as LUAD: significantly higher cytotoxicity (median 0.40 versus 0.31; BH-adjusted p < 0.001) and exhaustion (median 0.31 versus 0.22; p = 0.001) in non-MPR tumors, with stemness scores remaining near zero and not differing significantly (p = 0.175). This patient-level result was more consistent between histologies than the corresponding composition analysis, which had reached significance in LUSC but not in LUAD.
- Transcription factor activity inference identified only 14 of 649 tested transcription factors as significant (2.2 percent), despite a larger patient cohort than LUAD (93 versus 47 patients retained at the 10-cell CD8.TEX threshold: 29 MPR and 64 non-MPR in LUSC, 9 MPR and 38 non-MPR in LUAD) — a point to revisit in the Discussion once compared against the literature, rather than assumed to reflect a lack of statistical power. Of these, 13 were more active in non-MPR tumors and one, SNAI2, in MPR tumors. The dominant pattern involved E2F family members and TFDP1 (E2F4, E2F2, TFDP1, E2F1, associated with cell-cycle regulation), together with IRF3 and TBP. Ten of the 13 non-MPR transcription factors were also significant in LUAD (CEBPZ, E2F1, E2F4, EGR1, IRF3, JUN, NCOA3, RELA, STAT1, TFDP1), leaving only three specific to LUSC (E2F2, ZNF143, TBP): the LUSC signature is therefore largely a subset of the LUAD one, differing chiefly in which factors dominate rather than in their identity.

**TCR repertoire analysis (both histologies)**: neither clonal expansion nor Shannon diversity within CD8.TEX differed significantly between MPR and non-MPR tumors, in either histology, under any of the three analytical variants (primary within-TEX analysis, rarefaction to common depth, clone size defined over all T cells).

### Bloc7_TAM

- Functional scoring of the full macrophage lineage detected no difference between MPR and non-MPR tumors on three of the four axes in either histology. In LUAD (10 MPR, 39 non-MPR), only M1 survived correction (median 0.090 versus 0.115; adjusted P = 0.044), angiogenesis, M2 and phagocytosis showing no difference (adjusted P = 0.114, 0.443, 0.669). In LUSC (34 MPR, 62 non-MPR), M1 was likewise the only axis to differ, being lower in MPR tumors (median 0.075 versus 0.095; adjusted P = 0.015). On gating-confirmed cells alone the LUSC result held with a stronger signal (0.061 versus 0.094; adjusted P = 0.0019), the difference being distributed across the cohort rather than driven by outliers, with three quarters of MPR patients below the non-MPR median.

- Taken together, three characterisation frameworks were applied to this compartment and only one measure differed significantly, the M1 axis, robustly in LUSC alone. Abundance does not differ either: patient-level testing of the proportion of macrophages found no difference in either histology (LUAD 3.44 vs 4.27 %, adjusted P = 0.46; LUSC 1.23 vs 2.46 %, adjusted P = 0.65), with medians pointing in the same direction in both. Neither the state nor the number of macrophages distinguishes response groups in this cohort.

- The four published subtype markers did not distinguish response groups in either histology (LUAD adjusted P = 0.086 for both SPP1 and SELENOP, the closest; LUSC all above 0.19). Their distribution nonetheless differed markedly between histologies, and for FABP4 in the direction opposite to the source cohort: FABP4-assigned cells accounted for 2.5 percent of LUAD and 14.2 percent of LUSC macrophages here, against 35.6 and 7.3 percent respectively in untreated NSCLC. SPP1-assigned cells predominated in both histologies, particularly in LUAD at 66.3 percent, and a quarter of the compartment expressed none of the four markers. The sensitivity analysis behaved differently here than for the M1 axis: on the same nine and thirty-seven LUAD patients, widening the perimeter alone moved SPP1 from adjusted P = 0.293 to 0.125, the three additional patients contributing only the remaining shift to 0.086. Including transitional cells therefore strengthens the SPP1 contrast, consistent with their distinct profile, without changing the conclusion.

- The metabolic prediction was partially supported in LUSC and not in LUAD. Gene coverage reached 95 to 100 percent and no cell returned a zero score on any programme, so unlike the shorter signatures these measurements were not limited by detection. In LUSC, glycolysis was lower in responding tumors (0.0762 versus 0.0834; adjusted P = 0.011), in the predicted direction, while the other three programmes did not differ. In LUAD no programme reached significance, glycolysis coming closest (adjusted P = 0.178). The glycolysis result is driven by the perimeter rather than by the two patients it adds: testing the wider perimeter on the same thirty-two patients still gives adjusted P = 0.033, against 0.137 on gating-confirmed cells alone. On gating-confirmed cells the falsification criterion was met in both histologies; on the full lineage it is not, so the prediction should be reported as partially supported rather than refuted.

- The four metabolic programmes were not independent, and their coupling was similar across the two cohorts: fatty acid metabolism correlated with oxidative phosphorylation at rho = 0.67 in LUAD and 0.75 in LUSC, and with glycolysis at 0.58 and 0.72. Glycolysis correlated positively with oxidative phosphorylation (0.32 in LUAD, 0.53 in LUSC) although the two are expected to vary inversely, and only hypoxia behaved independently of the others (0.01 and −0.08 against oxidative phosphorylation). This stability across two histologically distinct cohorts, and across two perimeters, points to a property of rank-based per-cell scoring, whereby transcriptionally richer cells score highly on all programmes, rather than to a shared biological driver. The glycolysis difference detected in LUSC should be read with this in mind: it is the only programme to differ, but the programmes are not independent measures of metabolism.

- Taken together, five characterisation frameworks were applied to this compartment and only one measure differed significantly, the M1 axis in LUSC. Abundance does not differ either: patient-level testing of the proportion of macrophages found no difference in either histology (LUAD 3.44 vs 4.27 %, adjusted P = 0.46; LUSC 1.23 vs 2.46 %, adjusted P = 0.65), with medians pointing in the same direction in both. Neither the state nor the number of macrophages distinguishes response groups in this cohort.
  
- Transitional cells are not functionally neutral. Their M1 score is nearly identical to that of gating-confirmed cells (LUAD 0.113 versus 0.114; LUSC 0.092 versus 0.085) but their M2 score is lower (0.143 versus 0.182; 0.148 versus 0.178) and their angiogenesis score higher (0.116 versus 0.097; 0.116 versus 0.091), consistently across histologies. The signatures do not measure the same thing at different stages of differentiation.
- The perimeter does not drive the results, but the patient set does in LUAD. Three patients return with the wider perimeter (P533, P33, P382), all with unusually low purity — 40, 26 and 36 percent against group medians of 65.4 and 53.1. M1 reaches significance on the full perimeter (adjusted P = 0.044) but not when the same nine and thirty-seven patients are tested on that same perimeter (adjusted P = 0.116, against 0.084 on gating-confirmed cells only). In LUSC, where only two patients are added, M1 remains significant on both perimeters (adjusted P = 0.0019 on gating-confirmed cells, 0.0148 on the full lineage).

### Bloc8_Communication

- **Volume does not differ, organisation does.** Of the fifty highest ranking interactions per response group, those involving distinct cell groups numbered 31 and 31 in LUAD, and 27 and 28 in LUSC. After restricting to ligand-receptor pairs supported by at least two references, 17 and 24 remained in LUAD, 16 and 19 in LUSC. What separates the groups is the direction and content of the dialogue, not how much of it there is.

- **Inhibitory checkpoints are retained only among non-responders, in both histologies.** Seven of 24 filtered interactions in LUAD and five of 19 in LUSC targeted CTLA-4, PD-1, TIGIT or LAG3 on CD8.TEX cells. The three highest ranking were identical across cohorts: CD274→PDCD1, CD80→CTLA4, CD86→CTLA4. No inhibitory checkpoint interaction was retained among responders in either histology. Two cohorts, two histologies, the same pathways — this is the most reproducible result of the block.

- **The same ligands carry inhibition and co-stimulation.** CD80, CD86 and ICOSLG engaged CTLA-4 as well as CD28 and ICOS. This is expected, since CTLA-4 competes with CD28 for the B7 ligands, and should not be reported as two separate findings.

- **Signalling direction reverses in LUAD, less so in LUSC.** Along the CD8–macrophage axis in LUAD, 11 interactions ran from CD8.TEX to macrophages against 1 in the opposite direction among responders, and 2 against 9 among non-responders. All three inter-compartment axes ran from myeloid to lymphoid cells in non-responding LUAD. In LUSC the asymmetry was attenuated (8 against 4 among responders) and macrophage–macrophage interactions dominated instead, 18 and 14 of 50 against 7 and 4 in LUAD.

- **Emitters after filtering.** LUAD: CD8.TEX emitted 12 of 17 among responders, macrophages 16 of 24 among non-responders, monocytes 5 in both. LUSC: CD8.TEX 7, monocytes 5, macrophages 4 among responders; macrophages 12, CD8.TEX 5, monocytes 2 among non-responders. In LUAD, filtering removed every macrophage-emitted interaction among responders — they were extracellular matrix pairs with one reference or none.

- **Monocytes behave like macrophages in non-responders**, engaging the same checkpoints towards CD8.TEX (CD86→CTLA4, ICOSLG→CTLA4). Consistent with their position on the monocyte-to-macrophage continuum, and an argument for not treating them as a separate compartment in future analyses.

- **TGFB1 dominates among responders in both histologies**, 8 of 17 filtered interactions in LUAD and 5 of 16 in LUSC, emitted by CD8.TEX towards macrophages and engaging the same receptors (TGFBR1, TGFBR2, ENG, ACVRL1, ITGAV, ITGB1, APP). ⚠ Direct expression checking qualifies this: the ligand is not restricted to the sender compartment (LUAD 74.3 % of CD8.TEX vs 74.7 % of macrophages; LUSC 64.0 vs 77.9 %), so the assigned directionality reflects differential expression between response groups rather than a privileged cellular source. Receptors are predominantly myeloid (ITGAV 27.7 % of macrophages vs 4.4 % of CD8.TEX in LUSC; TGFBR1 29.8 vs 5.9 %; ACVRL1 absent from CD8.TEX). ITGB8 is near-absent from both compartments (5.4 and 0.4 %), so the TGFB1–ITGB8 interaction rests on a receptor present in a small minority of cells.

- **LUSC-specific features.** Three ADAM-family metalloproteases (ADAM10, ADAM17 → NOTCH2, ITGB1) — reported but not interpreted, since these cleave membrane proteins rather than acting as conventional ligands and ADAM17 alone has 90+ context-dependent substrates. TNF→TNFRSF1B among responders (26 references, the best documented interaction of the group), the receptor lacking a death domain. And among non-responders, CSF1 from CD8.TEX to monocytes engaging CSF1R: unlike TGFB1, expression supports the direction (CSF1 in 24.0 % of CD8.TEX in non-responders vs 14.6 % in responders; CSF1R in 77.6 % of macrophages and 40.6 % of monocytes vs 0.2 % of CD8.TEX). IFNG→IFNGR1/2 is similarly directional (IFNG in >50 % of CD8.TEX, <1.1 % of myeloid cells).

- **Detectability concentrates on the non-responder side across three independent analyses**: 4 and 1 transcription factors reached significance on the responder side (LUAD, LUSC) against 63 and 12; none of five phenotypic frameworks distinguished responders; inhibitory checkpoints were retained only among non-responders. Whether this reflects a microenvironment in which the stimulus has largely gone, or engagement that the present measurements do not capture, cannot be resolved here — the clonal repertoire was used to test the first reading in the CD8 compartment and did not support it.

- **Prioritisation ranks, it does not test.** No p-value is attached to a prioritised interaction. The score combines differential expression, cell type specificity and predicted ligand activity, each converted to a normalised rank. Interactions must be described as highly ranked, never as significant.
